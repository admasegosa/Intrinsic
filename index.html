Why INTRINSIC

Around 75% of EU residents already live in urban areas, and in Spain the share is expected to reach 88% by 2050. How people and goods move through cities shapes their climate impact, their air quality and the safety of everyone on the street.

Urban freight is a small share of traffic but has an outsized footprint, and the on-demand economy keeps pushing last-mile deliveries up. At the same time, cities now generate detailed mobility data from sensors, vehicles and phones, and new modes are arriving: delivery drones, connected and autonomous vehicles, and remote operation centres that supervise them.

INTRINSIC builds Artificial Intelligence methods that turn these opportunities into practical tools for logistics operators, transport authorities and traffic managers. The project draws on Machine Learning, hybrid metaheuristics, model-based optimisation and agentic AI.

Starting hypothesis

New AI methods that exploit the growing digitalisation of urban mobility, together with emerging services such as drone logistics and tele-operated automation, will help address the challenges facing urban mobility and freight transport in Europe and Spain.

  • 20–40%of CO₂ emissions from urban transport come from freight vehicles, which are only 15–25% of vehicle-kilometres.
  • +78%expected growth in last-mile delivery demand by 2030, driven by e-commerce and on-demand services.
  • ~20,400road deaths a year in the EU, 40% of them in urban areas.
  • Hundredsof simulation runs can be needed to design a single traffic control plan or congestion pricing scheme for a metropolitan area.

Three research lines, one toolkit

Each line extends work from earlier projects of the team (AI4Transport, LOGISTAR, SENATOR, TANGENT, MOMENTUM, SOTERIA, SYNCHROMODE and RENAISSANCE) into new territory.

AWork package 3

Drones and tele-operated vehicles in last-mile logistics

Delivery drones can skip road congestion, and tele-operated autonomous vans can share capacity between passengers and parcels. Planning fleets that combine them needs routing models that do not exist yet: no current vehicle routing model handles tele-operated vehicles together with the remote operators who assist them.

  1. Richer vehicle routing modelsBattery autonomy, no-fly zones, take-off and landing times for drones; operator shifts, network latency and restricted areas for tele-operated vehicles.
  2. Hybrid metaheuristics and hyperheuristicsLarge Neighbourhood Search hybrids and learning-based operator selection to solve the new models at realistic scale.
  3. Tests on real delivery dataInstances built from parcel delivery data from logistics partners, or standard benchmarks such as CVRPLIB.
BWork package 4

Agentic AI for mobility policy decisions

Designing a mobility policy usually means running large, slow traffic simulations many times. We are building a conversational decision support system where a planner can ask, for example, “find the best traffic control plan for the morning peak on a rainy day”, and an AI agent plans and runs the analysis.

  1. A tool suite behind an MCP serverOptimisation frameworks for traffic control, congestion pricing, demand-responsive transport, freight on transit and roadworks planning, coupled with simulators such as Aimsun and SUMO.
  2. An LLM orchestratorBreaks requests into analytical goals, chooses and sequences tools, checks its own inputs and outputs, and explains each decision.
  3. Systematic evaluationTask success, quality of goal decomposition, tool-selection accuracy and stability across repeated runs.
CWork package 5

Predicting road crash risk in real time

If a dangerous situation can be detected minutes before a crash, traffic managers can act. Current models raise too many false alarms and learn from crash records that are scarce and biased in what gets reported.

  1. Bias-aware synthetic crash dataGenerative AI conditioned on under-represented crash contexts, correcting for under-reporting in police records.
  2. Graph and Bayesian neural networksSpatio-temporal models over the road network that give well-calibrated crash probabilities and fewer false positives.
  3. Validation in MadridOpen crash and traffic data from the city of Madrid, combined with OpenStreetMap road infrastructure.

Software toolkit. The three lines meet in an integrated toolkit (work package 6), tested and validated on use cases so that logistics companies, administrations and researchers can put the results to work.
